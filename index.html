Shoulder Motion Guide

What your right shoulder may do at each stage, if you don't have surgery. Green = allowed. Red = not yet. Amber = carefully, or only with your physio. Your physio's plan comes first. If you have surgery, the clock restarts from the operation day.

1. Raise the arm forward

Seen from the side. Like reaching for a shelf in front of you.

2. Raise the arm to the side

Seen from the front. Like a jumping-jack arm.

3. Rotate the hand outward

Seen from above. Elbow at your side, bent 90°. Forearm swings from your belly outward.

4. The danger position

Seen from the front. Arm out to the side and rotated back, like throwing a ball. This is how the shoulder comes out.

Your activities at this stage

Check any motion: 5 questions

  1. Is my elbow in front of my body? In front is safe. Out to the side or behind is risky.
  2. Is my hand rotating outward or behind me? That is the danger direction.
  3. Is it within this stage's height and weight?
  4. Is it sudden? Catching, being pulled, slipping, reaching fast are riskier.
  5. Pain, or a feeling it might slip? Stop.

Traffic light

Go: elbow in front, below this stage's height, light, slow.
Careful: near this stage's limits. Slowly, a few times only.
Not yet: arm out and rotated back, heavy, fast, or painful.

General guidance for a first anterior dislocation treated without surgery. Angles and weights are typical, rounded values, not a prescription. Prepared with AI for one person's understanding; your orthopaedic surgeon and physiotherapist set your actual plan.
